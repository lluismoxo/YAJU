Meet the Software Factory

The Software Factory is the part of Yaju that takes work off your backlog and gives it back as something a person can review. This is the short introduction, for people who have heard the name and not much else.

Yaju Team · 14 June 2026

Most of what an engineering team wishes it had time for is not difficult. It is clear, small and permanently deprioritised, because there is always something more urgent than the dependency bump nobody wants to do.

The Software Factory exists for that pile.

The shape of it

You point it at a backlog item. It reads the issue, reads the surrounding code, plans a change, writes it, runs the tests, and opens a pull request describing what it did and why.

A person reviews that pull request the way they would review a colleague's. That is the whole interaction.

What it will not do

It does not merge. It does not decide a failing test is acceptable. It does not widen its own scope beyond the ticket it was given, which sounds like a small restraint and is the difference between a reviewable change and a sprawling one.

The boundary is enforced where the action happens rather than requested in an instruction, so it is a property of the system rather than an intention.

Why it sits on the platform rather than beside it

Standalone code-generation tools demo well and become difficult around week three, when there are forty of them running and nobody can say what they changed, what they cost or who owns the one that touched billing.

Because every Software Factory run is an ordinary agent run, it inherits what the platform already provides: spend attributed per agent, budgets that block, policy applied at the moment of action, evals scoring every run, and a complete audit trail.

None of that is a Software Factory feature. That is rather the point.

Where to start

Teams that get the most from it pick one category of well-specified, low-stakes work, run it there until the eval scores stop being interesting, and only then widen the boundary. Slower than the demo suggests, and the version that survives the first month.
